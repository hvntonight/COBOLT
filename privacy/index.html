PRIVACY POLICY

Privacy policy

Cobolt is a local-first private file safe for macOS. This policy explains how the current Cobolt macOS app, website, and support pages handle information.

Effective: August 17, 2026　·　Last updated: August 17, 2026

At a glance

We designed Cobolt's core workflow to be local-first: your files, vault catalogs, and unlock flow run on your Mac. Cobolt does not require an account, sync core files to a Cobolt cloud, or run telemetry or advertising tracking for the core task.

◎

In one sentence: You give a file to Cobolt so it can stay on your device—not so we can hold it for you.

1. Information in the Cobolt app

What you put in a vault

Files you choose to import, vault names, file names, sizes, types, dates, other catalog metadata, and local vault settings are stored on your Mac. Cobolt uses local authenticated encryption for vault contents; core files are not uploaded to Cobolt servers.

Local references and activity data

To support Restore to original location, Cobolt can store local source paths and security-scoped bookmarks for imported items. The app also keeps local activity events, integrity-check results, and—when you use them—encrypted-backup destinations and version metadata. These records stay on your Mac.

Information used to unlock and protect a vault

Your password derives a wrapping key that unlocks a randomly generated vault master key. Touch ID is handled by macOS security APIs and the optional key material is protected by the macOS Keychain. A recovery key can restore access when you enable it. Passwords, master keys, and decrypted file bytes do not leave the native macOS app through the web bridge.

Temporary decrypted copies

Quick Look, Finder handoff, and other system flows that need to show content may require a temporary plaintext copy. Cobolt lets you review tracked copies in Preferences and works to remove them when you lock or quit, but deletion from an SSD is not the same as forensic-grade erasure. On a shared Mac, also follow macOS account and disk-security practices.

Diagnostics, advertising, and accounts

The current core workflow does not require a Cobolt account, run Cobolt advertising tracking, or send core operations to a Cobolt telemetry service. If a future version adds analytics, crash reporting, cloud sync, or another data practice, we will update this policy before that feature ships and provide choices where appropriate.

2. Purchases and Apple

Cobolt Pro uses Apple StoreKit for purchases and restores. Apple processes payment-card information; Cobolt does not receive your complete payment details. The app reads the product or entitlement state Apple returns to decide whether to enable Pro features.

Apple may handle App Store, StoreKit, and device-related information under its own policies. See Apple's Privacy Policy and Apple Media Services Terms and Conditions.

3. Website and third parties

This website is a static GitHub Pages site. Cobolt does not set advertising cookies, require a login, or run product analytics here. GitHub may still process standard access logs, security information, and hosting data under its terms and privacy statement. See GitHub's Privacy Statement.

Apple, GitHub, and other links on these pages take you to third-party sites; their practices are governed by their own privacy notices.

4. Retention, export, and deletion

Vaults and files remain on your Mac until you delete them in Cobolt or Finder, or remove the app data. Encrypted backups and password-protected share packages that you create are separate local copies and must be deleted at their chosen destinations. Cobolt does not have an online account that stores these files, so there is no Cobolt server account-file deletion request.

If you delete a vault, confirm that you have backed up anything you still need. When a password is lost without a recovery key, the vault is intentionally not recoverable; that is a security boundary, not a support process Cobolt can bypass.

5. Contact us

For privacy or data-processing questions, contact the maintainer through Cobolt GitHub Issues. Please do not upload passwords, recovery keys, identity documents, or sensitive files to a public issue.

We will update this page when product features or data practices materially change, and update the effective date at the top.
